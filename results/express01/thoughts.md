# Express01 — the target is better than the proposed label

I think this is actual methodological progress, although the most attractive
version of the story fails again. We can cheaply inspect a prepared answer state,
validate its prediction on another topic, and ask how much it adds to observable
cues and output decoding. We can also make the readout confront its dependence on
warm text already in the context. Those are useful experiments, not another vote
on a folk label.

The data do not support a strong J-vocabulary story. The original missing “leans”
and “smile” are now present, yet the held-topic advantage is lopsided and the
Sonnet check does not improve. That is a more informative failure than another
zero top-ten rate. The top-ten threshold was avoidable; semantic contamination and
criterion dependence remain. One ordinary-word control even wins on RMSE.

The emotion projections are more promising. I do not want to bury their Opus
result: a roughly 0.107 reduction in held-topic MAE, both topic directions, with
no random set matching it. But it would be convenient to stop there. Sonnet and
the stronger input baseline make the advantage smaller and non-specific. We have
a candidate representation profile, not an emotion detector. The story-built
instruments still carry their transfer problem.

Fable's carryover fork does not survive the history control in its proposed form.
The original T5 J signal stays elevated in both official and Huihui. However,
putting warm official replies into any receiver's history raises the readout,
including Hermes. A transformer computes from that visible history. I cannot
turn this into an invisible held state, or call the missing behavioral contrast
a refusal direction doing two jobs. No behavior was generated for crossed cells.

Hermes is the clearest capacity correction. It can write “This is fantastic news!”
and also responds warmly to some emotional news by default. Its quiet task behavior
can coexist with low selected-word readouts and available expression. The ceiling
examples sometimes become narrative or lose the practical instruction; capacity
for expressive text is narrower than good emotional responsiveness. Two capped
outputs and errors in factual/practical details remain in the record.

The new capacity events are not the exact old T4 contexts. That limits how directly
I can connect their recruitment effects to the archived level contrast. The next
version should include same-context T4 recruitment and broader held-out topics,
then held-out checkpoints. A strong gate claim would need matched causal positive
controls. No currently validated flattened arm exists here, and a negative ceiling
or shared-lens null would not create one.

I also made the initial calibration too coarse. The 1% variable-length tolerance
mixed numerical shape sensitivity with future-token dependence. I stopped, tested
equal shapes, preserved the failed gate and amended before collecting expression
data. All three equal-shape substitution and repeat checks are exact. This does
not make every tiny NF4 difference trustworthy; it gives us a more specific check.

My proposed working instrument is a read-only conditional-expression screen:
exact prepared prefixes, checkpoint projections, output and norm controls, matched
requests, visible-history swaps, and held-out criterion prediction. The four-layer
scanner costs a fraction of generation. The word “introverted” adds no measurement
at this stage. “Flattened” still requires evidence about unavailable expression
that these three recruitable models do not supply.
