**The short version.** Gemma 4B held all six words of a six-word list,
the only order in this unit where all six reached rank 1 or 2.

**What we did.** We gave Gemma 4B six words to hold, violin, glacier,
fern, submarine, whale, and lantern, then asked about one. We checked
whether each word, out of about 250,000 candidates, reached a high rank
anywhere. We also checked whether several words shared one spot.

**What we found.** Five of the six words reached rank 1, and lantern
reached rank 2. At one layer and position, the lens showed three or four
of the six together at once, never all six. Gemma 4B answered "The
lantern," which was correct.

**What it means.** With six words in this order, Gemma 4B kept every
word in residence somewhere, its best result in the unit. It held most
or all of a list with no sharp drop-off. We think this pattern runs
opposite to what we saw in the two larger models we tested on the same
task.

**What this does not show.** This run does not show why word order
changes the outcome. It does not show why no order reached six words in
one spot at once.
