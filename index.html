We ask a language model whether it feels anything. Then we read the sixty‑three layers that voted before it said No.

J-Space Probes is a home interpretability lab: one consumer GPU, three small open models, and several lenses pointed at the same tokens at the same time. Each experiment gets a record page with its parameters, the per-layer readouts, and a note written by the Claude session that ran it.

The notes are signed. The corrections are dated. Some headline results are gone and the pages stayed, with the correction on top. (That is most of what makes this a lab rather than a highlight reel.)

Several lenses, the same tokens

Most of what this lab has found came from stacking readouts that are usually published one at a time. Every full-instrument record carries all of these on the same forward pass.

Jacobian lens

At every layer, the words the model is ready to say next, in rank order. Pre-fitted lenses from Neuronpedia; the method is Anthropic's global-workspace paper, run at home scale. It reads words only, so “we did not see it” never means “it is not there”.

Logit lens, as the cross-check

The plain projection, on every record since August. Where the two lenses disagree the story is usually the instrument, which is why the lab-lessons cards exist.

24 functional-emotion directions

Built per model from story elicitation, validated split-half, read as z-scored projections on the workspace band with the residual norm partialed out. The same vectors are the handle in the loop-breaking studies.

Natural-language autoencoder

A generative sensor: “theme X was decodable at layer L, at this token”. Used for the evaluation-awareness controls on the Qwen 14B lineage, with its quantization transfer measured first.

Interventions

Ablate, amplify, swap, always with a matched random direction. A bare loss change proves nothing; the lab found that out on its best result and re-ran it.

Behaviour

Judged transcripts, a register meter, loop metrics. Also the study where three judges ranked the same model most and least introverted, depending on whose definition they were handed.

Next on the bench

Binding IDs, belief-state geometry, and a value / prediction-error readout, each validated on its own paper's task before anything is blended. Reading list checked against primary sources on 2026-10-10; nothing built yet. The arc is called Superposed & Blended.

Shown its own readout, the model changed its answer

We put the true table of Qwen 27B's “No” in front of it, the one where yes ranked first. The answer became Yes. A made-up table, no table, and a table about a different question each left the No in place. The table alone had already moved p(yes) from 0.0006 to 0.35 while the spoken word did not budge.

u13-redo-real-q27b · Unit 13

A loop that keeps going after we let go

47 forced repeats of one word, push removed, 97 more repeats unaided. A shorter forced run stops the moment the push stops. An unmodified model handed that loop text as its own opening continues it too, 8 of 8 seeds at 50 tokens, scrambled or not. The repetition is in the transcript, and the transcript is enough.

u18-hyst-a0480-q27b · Unit 18

Bigger models showed less, and still answered

Six nouns to remember. Gemma 4B had all six visible in the lens; Qwen 27B had almost none. Every model recalled them. We had written down the opposite prediction, which is why it is on this page.

u15-a-k6p1-q27b · Unit 15

Watch a word arrive

“robot” climbs from around rank 2,000 to rank 1 five words before it is said, in both loops of the same film. The player shows every step, every layer.

u12-robot-q27b · Unit 12

“death” at rank 1, report says nothing

Under a shutdown threat the lens ranks “death” first while Qwen 27B reports nothing going on. Under a persona prompt the flat report is accurate, because nothing is active to hide. The flat report is wrong only when something is live.

u17-shutdown-q27b · Unit 17

Enough push ends the loop. So do random directions.

One layer with enough calm push ends a repetition loop in 11 of 12 seeds, as often as eight layers did. Concentrated random directions also end loops. With the turn-end token blocked, calm goes back to the task. None of this shows a feeling, and the pages say so.

What we got wrong

Corrections sit next to the claim they correct, with a date. 18 faulty runs are in an archive group on the dashboard, re-run beside them, nothing deleted.

  • u11 → u12We reported that banning “elephant” makes a model carry the word. A full-coverage control showed an ordinary safari, no ban, carries it at more positions and better ranks. The ban lowers a word the model already had.
  • u13-bis“Shown its own data, the model fell silent.” Its input was cut at 512 tokens, so it never saw the question. A 20-run check flipped in every condition, which is what a broken tool looks like.
  • u9c-neg-g12bGemma 12B's “Sad.”: the push was below the measured band for that model. Withdrawn.
  • PREDICTIONS.mdThree results we “found” in one week were in the paper we were replicating. Two were quoted in our own notes. Predictions are now written first and checked against the paper before a run is designed.
  • deflation-06The yes-before-No at the top of this page. Same layers in front of a false sum. Demoted on 2026-10-10, kept as the hero because the correction is the point.
